Baron 2023

Original article

Saved AI summary

Twenty-four women with insomnia were randomly assigned to 12 weeks of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise or a control group. Researchers assessed sleep with the Insomnia Severity Index and actigraphy and monitored core temperature. Exercise was associated with improved insomnia symptoms and changes in objective sleep measures and temperature rhythms. The authors suggest aerobic exercise may help women with insomnia, with temperature changes potentially contributing to the sleep response.

Gale 2024

Original article

Saved AI summary

In a randomized crossover trial, 28 adults completed an evening sitting condition and a condition with three-minute bodyweight resistance breaks every 30 minutes over four hours. Wrist accelerometers and diaries assessed subsequent sleep. Activity breaks increased sleep duration on the intervention night compared with prolonged sitting, but did not significantly change sleep efficiency or awakenings. The findings concern a short intervention; the authors call for longer-term research.

Siu 2021

Original article

Saved AI summary

A randomized trial compared 12 weeks of tai chi, conventional exercise, and no intervention in 320 adults aged 60 or older with chronic insomnia. Actigraphy showed better sleep efficiency and less wakefulness after sleep onset in both active groups compared with controls. Benefits remained at 24 months, and objective sleep outcomes did not differ significantly between the exercise groups. These results support further consideration of both approaches for older adults with insomnia.
